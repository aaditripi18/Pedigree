Hub lineage audit · snapshot 2026-11-04

Do models come from the model they say they do?

A model card's base_model is how licence checks, procurement reviews and the Hub itself learn what a model is built on. Nobody checks it. Pedigree checks it from the weights, on a frozen random sample of 600 Hub models, with a verifier measured on ground truth first.

01The problem

A declared base is a claim nobody checks

A fine-tune of a model with a restrictive licence can be re-uploaded under a permissive one with no parent named; a card can name a base the model does not descend from. The Hub shows the claim as fact.

Weight-based lineage methods already exist and are strong (AWM, ICLR 2026; HuRef, NeurIPS 2024; MoTHer, ICLR 2025). Pedigree does not claim a new detector. It uses that idea as an instrument, measures the instrument on ground truth first, and then measures the Hub, with every hypothesis and decision rule committed in PLAN.md before its data existed.

  1. 1
    Read, don't download. File headers and the first values of up to 24 tensors, by HTTP range request: a 70B model costs megabytes.
  2. 2
    Align. Pair tensors by name and shape (GGUF, MLX, PyTorch and safetensors names mapped to one form).
  3. 3
    Decide. Median cosine over aligned tensors against a threshold fixed on development negatives only: 0.0255.
02Ground truth first

The instrument, measured before it measures the Hub

Thresholds come from development pairs only: the highest unrelated score plus five standard deviations, wide because the audit makes thousands of comparisons. Then each was judged once on pairs it never saw, across six families (two compared blind, after the rule was written).

03The Hub audit (E2)

What 600 random Hub models declare, and what their weights show

H2NOT SUPPORTED

Undeclared lineage is common, but under the bar

105 of 364 models whose weights show an earlier public relative declare no base (28.8%; weighted by tier size 19.8%). The bar was 30%. A finding about the Hub, not a fault of the instrument.

H3NOT SUPPORTED

When a card names a base, it is almost always right

3 of 227 comparable declared bases are contradicted (1.3%, interval 0.4%-3.8%); in each the weights show no lineage at all. The bar was 5%: the Hub's declarations are more honest than the hypothesis guessed.

After four reader fixes (MLX bit packing, GGUF names in multimodal checkpoints, pre-2020 PyTorch files, GGUF tensor types decoded from their public sources) the same 600 models were scored again: no verdict changes (H2 106/379 undeclared; H3 3/279 contradicted; H7 (a) 20/24; (b) 88/250).

04Finding a parent nobody declared

The candidate pool, rebuilt from its own misses

To find an undeclared parent, each model is compared with a pool of candidates. E2's pool held the declared parent for only 76 of 199 Hub models (H7, not supported). Every miss had a recorded cause; pool v2 answers each without reading the model's own declaration, and was judged once on 216 fresh models drawn before any was read.

H13a supported; H13b supported (exact McNemar p = 2.6e-30), at a mean of 54 candidates per model.

05The second instrument

Behaviour, where weights cannot be compared

A distilled student shares no weights with its teacher, so behaviour was pre-registered for those cases. Four hypotheses tested it on fresh students; none passed (H9, H12, H14, H14b). The data shows why: within each training batch the vision-CNN students do separate, but the whole fresh batch sits below the threshold chosen on the dev batch, so an absolute threshold does not carry from one batch to the next. H16 compares each student with reference students trained the same way instead; H16b trains text-encoder and vision-transformer students long enough to learn the task.

On the Hub, behaviour applied to none of the 94 declared pairs weights could not compare (28 with neither a shared language-model output nor a shared label set; 66 with a model above 2B parameters). It runs on larger models (H18) only if H16 or H16b passes first: behaviour on a 7B model proves nothing until the behaviour check itself has passed on ground truth.

07Scope

Every caveat, measured

Each weakness found along the way is either fixed and re-tested on fresh data, or measured, with what is being done about it.

Declared bases weights cannot compare

35 models (36 pairs) after the reader fixes: 14 GGUF: no tensor name maps; 10 declared base not readable when read again (missing, renamed or not a repo id); 7 other format (no shared shapes, draft head, repack, no weights); 5 gated or private.

→ H17 aligns GGUF tensors by block and unique shape when names fail. Against name alignment on fresh pairs (H17a, not supported) it agreed on 279/339, gave the opposite verdict on 0 and aligned nothing on 60; H17b: not supported. GGUF types newer than the gguf package are decoded only from their public sources; gated bases are read through a byte-identical public copy.

The candidate pool

E2's pool missed the parent of 123 of 199 Hub models; every miss has a recorded cause (results/h7_misses.json).

→ Pool v2 holds 196 of 216 fresh parents (H13a supported).

Direction: which model is the parent

Hub dates are right on 183/193 pairs; 9 of the 10 misses are one organisation's joint releases, where the derived repo was created at most 7.9 days before its base. Kurtosis is right on 35/39 decided pairs and leaves 20/59 undecided (short fine-tunes and LoRA merges barely move it). H8: not supported.

→ D3 (dates across owners, names within one release, kurtosis last): right on 184/189 decided dev pairs; on the fresh sample (H15, not supported) 222/237 decided, no better than dates alone (226/240); kurtosis is right on only 22/118 fresh Hub pairs, the reverse of its assumed direction. The audit keeps dates.

Behaviour as evidence of lineage

Absolute thresholds do not transfer between training batches (vision CNN: fresh TPR 5/5 at FPR 5/5); text-encoder and vision-transformer students never learned the task in 2,000 steps.

→ H16: reference-calibrated rule, fixed on dev (supported); H16b: students trained to a competence floor (not decidable).

Behaviour above 2B parameters

66 of the declared pairs weights could not compare have a model above 2B parameters; behaviour was not run on them because no behaviour rule has passed on ground truth.

→ H18 runs on text models up to 14B only if H16 or H16b is supported (not judged); most of these pairs are GGUF files that H17 may make comparable by weights instead.

The threshold rule

Under the pre-registered sensitivity rule (midpoint between the classes) 4 verdicts change: H3, H10, H11, H28a.

→ The published rule is max + 5 SD; both columns are in docs/RESULTS.md.

Hub drift during the audit

Pass 2 re-read 457 models independently: 429 identical; the others changed in their commit, declaration or pool.

→ Each model keeps its first reading (results/e2_rereads.json).

Sampled tensors vs whole files

Whole-file and sampled verdicts agree on 75/75 fresh pairs and 50/50 Hub pairs; largest score difference 0.074.

→ The threshold sits far from both classes, so that difference never moves a verdict.

08Reproduce

Every number, from the ledger

from a clone of the repo 
# install, run the tests
$ pip install -e ".[dev,experiments]" && pytest
# check one model against its declared base_model, or a base you name
$ pedigree verify hf:org/model
$ pedigree verify hf:org/model --base hf:org/base
# every verdict from results/ledger.jsonl, then this site
$ python scripts/analyse.py
$ python scripts/build_site.py

The heavy runs (ground-truth fine-tunes on GPU, the 600-model audit on CPU) ran on Kaggle with scripts/kaggle_run.py, which uploads only committed code and stamps every result with its commit. CI reruns the analysis and the site build and fails on any difference.
